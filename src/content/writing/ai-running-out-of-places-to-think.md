# AI Isn't Running Out of Ideas... It's Running Out of Places to Think

Every time you ask an AI model a question, somewhere in the world a data centre wakes up. 

GPUs spin into life. Electricity is consumed. Water cools the hardware. Fibre cables carry your request across continents before an answer finds its way back to your screen.

Every AI conversation begins in a place you'll probably never see. Not on your laptop or inside ChatGPT, but inside a warehouse filled with thousands of GPUs, kilometres of fibre optic cables and cooling systems working around the clock just to keep those chips alive. 

Before an AI model answers your question, it has already consumed electricity, compute power and network infrastructure that most of us never think about.

## Who Owns the Infrastructure Intelligence Depends On?

I found one section of Kenya's 2026 AI and Emerging Technologies Policy surprisingly refreshing. 

Instead of asking how Kenya can build the next breakthrough AI model, it spends a lot of time asking a much more fundamental question: **who owns the infrastructure that intelligence depends on?**

Because if every serious AI product still relies on foreign cloud providers, imported GPUs, and compute hosted thousands of kilometres away, then we're not just importing technology; we're importing the foundations that make it possible.

## Changing the Equation: The Five Policy Pillars

The policy tries to change that equation. It proposes:

- **Compute Credits**: Allowing local startups to afford real model experimentation.
- **Sovereign Cloud Infrastructure**: Protecting critical national workloads on domestic hardware.
- **Green Compute Zones**: Powered directly by Kenya's abundant geothermal energy grid.
- **Investment Pipelines**: Attracting capital for new domestic AI data centres.
- **Tariff Reforms**: Lowering import duties to make enterprise GPUs cheaper to deploy locally.

None of these ideas will make headlines on their own, but together they reveal a different strategy. Kenya isn't only trying to attract AI companies; it's trying to become the place where AI can actually be built.

## The Story Is Leverage

The more I read, the more I realised this isn't really a story about data centres or cloud infrastructure. It's a story about **leverage**.

Countries that shape the future of AI won't necessarily be the ones with the biggest models. They'll be the ones that make it easier, cheaper, and more sustainable for everyone else to build them.

Reading this policy, I got the sense that Kenya is quietly positioning itself for exactly that race.
